003 · Beyond the base card

Showcase cards

A broad category given to Alternate Art and Overnumber cards after the Origins release — different in treatment and playability rules.

Alternate Arts

Fall within the set's normal card count and are legal in tournament as long as the base card is legal. The main champions get an alternate-art version of their Champion Unit for each domain they're assigned — the champion in another skin, with a more extravagant foil.

Overnumber

Falls outside the set's normal card count — artist-commissioned styles and premium foil/UV treatments. These do not count as a reprint and are the hardest single pulls in any box (see the pull-rate table further down).

004 · Pick a philosophy

The six domains

Riftbound is built around six domains — philosophies of power each champion aligns with (or against). Every deck is built around one or two.

005 · Learn to play

Turns, terms & keywords

The core rules vocabulary every Riftbound match runs on — plus every new keyword, token, and mechanic each set has added since Origins.

Key game terms

Core keywords

Symbols on the card

A quick key to the icons printed on Riftbound cards and in their text boxes.

Card cost symbols

Wild Power

Recycle any one domain rune as Power.

Energy cost

Pay this much Energy by exhausting any runes.

Flow (Trash)

Appears on cards with the Flow keyword. It shows the card has an ability while in your trash.

It stays visible when cards are fanned face-down, so players can see active trash effects at a glance.

In-text symbols

Might

A unit's combat strength, or a modifier to it (like +2).

It appears on unit cards and shows how powerful the unit is in combat. A higher Might number means the unit deals more damage and is harder to remove from a battlefield. It's one of the most important numbers on any unit card.

Exhaust sometimes called “Tap”

Cards, runes and legends exhaust, or turn sideways, to move or use abilities. They can’t exhaust again until readied.

Recycle paying Power

Recycle doesn’t have an icon of its own. A Power cost is shown with a colored domain symbol — here Fury, Calm, Body, Order, Mind and Chaos — and you pay it by recycling a rune of that domain, putting it on the bottom of the appropriate deck. The rainbow Wild Power symbol lets you recycle a rune of any domain.

That’s different from Energy, which is the number in a circle and is paid by exhausting runes. Some effects also tell you to recycle cards from your trash or other zones, which moves them to the bottom of their deck.

Power Activation

Also called an activated ability. Exhaust one rune of that specific domain to activate the ability.

Reading a power cost

3 Energy + 1 Fury rune

Requires 3 Energy plus 1 Fury rune to play.

1 Energy + 2 Body runes

Requires 1 Energy plus 2 Body runes to play.

Either / or cost

Requires 1 Energy plus 1 Fury rune or 1 Energy plus 1 Body rune to play.

Legend cards

The two domain icons in the top left of a Legend card show which domains it belongs to, which defines the card types you can include in your deck.

Reading the effect box

Adding Energy

Add 2 Energy to your pool this turn.

Energy plus a rune

Pay 1 Energy and recycle 1 Fury rune, then return a unit from your trash to your Main Deck. Exhaust this card. If you do, play a 3 Might Mech unit token to your base.

Deflect

Deflect 2 means Deflect with a value of 2, and the two wild powers shown are the cost opponents must pay to target the unit.

Gold token ability

Destroy this Gold token as the cost (the token must be ready). As a Reaction, you immediately add 1 Power to your resource pool. Because this ability adds resources directly, your opponent can’t react to it — the Power is added instantly.

Accelerate

Pay 1 Energy AND recycle 1 Fury rune as the additional Accelerate cost.

Equip

Recycle 1 Calm rune to attach this equipment to a unit you control.

Repeat

Repeat (1) means you may pay the additional cost to repeat the spell’s effect once. Some spells can be repeated any number of times — for example, each repeat costing 4 Energy and 1 Mind rune.

Assault

Assault 2 means the unit gets +2 Might while attacking.

Scoring ability

Pay 4 Energy AND recycle 4 Mind runes, then exhaust this card to score 1 point.

Might bonus box

Shows how much Might a gear card adds to the unit it’s attached to.

Going deeper: keywords with symbols

Hunt & Level introduced in Unleashed (Set 3, May 2026)

Hunt 2 means this unit gains 2 XP when it conquers or holds a battlefield. XP builds up for your Legend across the whole game and raises your level as it grows. Level 3 means the unit gains the stated effect permanently while your Legend has 3 or more XP — no spending required, it’s always active once you meet the threshold. On Mosstomper that is +1 Might and Deflect, so opponents must pay a Wild Power cost to target it with a spell or ability. Hunt builds your XP; Level abilities unlock automatically once you have enough.

Ambush introduced in Unleashed (Set 3, May 2026)

Ambush means this unit can be played as a Reaction to a battlefield where you already have units. You can’t drop into an empty battlefield — you need presence there first. Individual cards can extend this: Rengar, Trophy Hunter can Ambush into any battlefield, even without your units there, because his card text overrides the base keyword.

Empower Vendetta

Empower appears on some Vendetta cards as an activated ability. Pay the cost shown on that card, and it becomes Empowered for as long as it stays in play, gaining access to its Empowered abilities. Punching Poro’s Empower cost is discarding 1 card from your hand, and it gains +1 Might while Empowered. Each card can only be Empowered once per game, and it stays Empowered until it leaves play or is Disempowered by another effect.

Flow

Flow appears on spell cards as an alternative way to play them. Instead of playing the card from hand at its normal cost, you can pay the Flow cost to play it directly from your trash. After it resolves, the card is banished permanently. For example, Flow 2 means you pay 2 Energy to play the card from your trash; if its effect is Draw 1, you draw a card for 2 Energy straight from the trash. The Flow symbol printed above and left of the text box stays visible when your trash is fanned face-down, so you and your opponent can see a card there has an active ability.

Source: RiftboundGuide.com card-reading guides.

Equipment

Equipment is a kind of gear that enhances and empowers your units. Every Equipment card has a new frame with two text boxes — while unattached, only the upper box is active (usually including EQUIP); once attached, the lower box takes over and is granted to the unit it's on, covering (and disabling) the upper box.

EQUIP Pay the listed cost to attach this to a unit you control.

Tokens

Tokens represent units and gear created during play by some Riftbound cards. They can be represented by anything you have handy, as long as it's clear where they are and whether they're ready or exhausted. Once played, a token is treated just like any other card — except it stops existing the moment it leaves the board.

Deck building

Every deck is built from the same five pieces, and all of them (except your Legend itself) must share its Domain Identity.

Your deck may also include up to 3 Signature cards total that share your Champion Legend's tag, regardless of name — Signature cards aren't Champion Units and can't go in the Champion Zone.

Match structure

Being tested: start-of-game rules

Playtest only — not a rules change. Riot says going first currently wins more often than it should, and the extra rune the second player channels isn’t enough to even it out. It is testing new start-of-game steps to bring the split closer to 50/50. Nothing changes at your Nexus Night, Regionals, or other events unless an official update is announced.
Test procedure (best of 3)
1. Turn order

Decide who goes first and second — now before battlefields.

2. Battlefields

Each player picks a battlefield knowing the turn order and places it face down.

3. Sideboard

Games 2 and 3 only.

4. Reveal battlefields
5. Mulligan
6. First player

Channels 2 runes and draws 1.

7. Second player

Channels 3 runes, then gets the compensation being tested.

Best of 1: same idea, but battlefields stay random. You see your own battlefield before sideboarding; your opponent’s is revealed after.

Option A

The second player starts the game with 1 point.

Option B

The second player draws an extra card.

Option C

The second player may Predict before their first draw — a softer version of Option B.

Where & when

Radiance-season City Tournaments in China, and the Friday and Sunday Standard Challenges at the European Regional Championship in Stuttgart. Staff announce which option is used before each event. Premier events, Skirmishes, and Showdowns don’t use it unless organizers are told to; casual players are welcome to try it. Riot will decide before Legacy launches and explain the result in the Legacy patch notes.

Source: Riot’s Testing Start of Game Rules announcement (October 15, 2026).

Regional product differences — French & Korean

The sealed products above are English-language releases. French and Korean launched later and are catching up on accelerated schedules, so which products exist, what’s inside them, and when they arrive can differ from English — and plans can change. Treat this as a reference and check Riot’s announcements before buying.

  • French: Origins (Origines) launched in French on May 29, 2026 with boosters, Champion Decks, and Proving Grounds. Spiritforged (Armes Spirituelles) and Unleashed (Déchaînement) followed together, and French is due to reach parity with English and Chinese at Radiance in October 2026. The French roadmap currently lists boosters, Champion Decks, Proving Grounds, and a Radiance Showdown Deck — it doesn’t mention Vaults or blister packs. French stores can keep ordering English product during the catch-up.
  • Korean: Korean launched September 18, 2026 with Origins only: Origins boosters, a separate five-card Origins booster, Origins Champion Decks, and Proving Grounds. Korean sets are planned to reach the global schedule by the start of 2028 (seven Korean sets in 2027), and a temporary “Korean Standard” format grows with each release.
Sources: Riot’s French roadmap and Korean launch announcements.

Organized play changes in 2027

Riot is renaming and expanding the competitive ladder for 2027. Names elsewhere in this handbook reflect the current season.

Nexus Cup — new

A new monthly local game store tournament program for recurring community competition outside Premier Play.

Riftbound Challenge Series

Replaces Summoner Skirmishes as the local on-ramp and base rung of the competitive ladder, held once per set.

Riftbound Regionals

Replaces Regional Qualifiers, with more events and more global locations through the year (Madrid and Philadelphia are listed for early 2027). These are 3-day events.

Riftbound Continentals

Replaces Regional Championships as the cornerstone event tied to each set release, held at four global destinations (Dallas in March 2027, for example). Three days, with Day 1 an open qualifier for players who weren’t invited.

Official store event registration is also moving from Carde.io to PlayRiftbound.com from late 2026 into 2027. Source: Riot’s early 2027 Premier Play announcement.

China & France spotlight

Two of Riftbound's most distinct regional scenes — different tournament structures, different prize walls, different metas.

China Events
France Events

China hosts the largest and most highly funded tier of physical Riftbound competitive play globally — large player turnouts, highly structured convention-scale infrastructure, and its own prize-wall culture built around licensed merchandise rather than flat prize cards.

English cards are manufactured and published globally by UVS Games; Chinese editions are handled regionally by Shining Soul. The physical differences are real and affect value.
